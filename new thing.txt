buffer is at ebp-0x18, flag is at ebp-0x8
Distance: 0x18 - 0x8 = 0x10 = 16 bytes. So strcpy fills buffer[0..15] with the first 16 bytes of your input, then anything from the 17th byte onward spills into flag’s 4 bytes.

flow.c
lea eax,[ebp-0x18]      <- this is where "buffer" lives
cmp DWORD PTR [ebp-0x8],0x41614262   <- this is where "pattern" lives, and what it must equal
0x18 0x8 diff is 10 hex = 16 dec -> 16 filler for buffer then 4 for pattern (int) (0x41614262)
Computers store numbers backwards (this is just a fact of x86 computers, called “little-endian”). The number 0x41614262 is made of 4 bytes: 41 61 42 62. Stored backwards in memory, that’s 62 42 61 41. (bBaA)
./pattern.out AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAbBaA
